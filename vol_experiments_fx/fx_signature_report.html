Capstone results · EUR/USD hourly data, Jan 2012 – Oct 2026

Do path signatures carry over from Bitcoin to FX?

On Bitcoin, signatures of the hourly price path improved volatility forecasts by 5–15% over the standard HAR model, and a signature anomaly detector passed our test at one day. We ran the same experiments on EUR/USD, with three channels built for FX, to see whether those results carry over.

Partly. The method carries over; the gain over HAR does not.

On EUR/USD the signature is again a much better summary of the hourly path than the raw path itself, but it does not beat a HAR forecast. The reason looks like the data, not the method. On EUR/USD, HAR fed with hourly realized variance doesn't beat plain daily HAR either. So at 1 and 5 days the hourly path holds little that the daily volatility history doesn't already contain.

The first row is the part that transfers. On both assets, the same information fed in as raw lags does 6–14% worse, so the signature's way of summarizing a path helps on both. What changes on FX is that the path itself carries little forecasting information. A neural network on the signature did much worse than ridge on both assets (−59% and −42% versus HAR on EUR/USD), so it is not a way out.

Finding 3 · FX-specific channels

The new FX channels made the forecasts slightly worse

We added two things to the path that FX traders would expect to matter: returns adjusted for the time of day (EUR/USD is far more volatile at the London and New York opens than in the Asian afternoon) and the bid-ask spread relative to its usual level for that hour. Same signature model and test days; only the channels change.

The original channels (price, activity, range) work best, and on these they nearly tie with HAR at 1 day (+1.7%). The time-of-day adjustment costs about 1.5% and the spread a little more. The adjustment probably removes a pattern that HAR can't see and the signature could. Adjusted price alone, without activity and range, does much worse. Depth 2 signatures did as well as depth 3, and the lead-lag transform added nothing over the plain path.

Finding 5 · Anomaly detection

Signature anomaly scores don’t predict EUR/USD volatility surprises

A day counts as a surprise when realized volatility lands in the top 20% relative to the HAR forecast. A detector earns credit only for flagging those, not for finding days that are simply volatile. The test: the best signature detector, picked on the first two folds, must beat the best signature-free detector on the last fold.

Surprise AUC on the last (held-out) fold, within bands of the HAR forecast; 0.5 = no information beyond HAR. p: block bootstrap, one-sided.

  • The detectors can see path shapes: on synthetic windows where only the order of returns is changed, the selected signature detectors score AUC 0.86–0.95. The failure is in the data, not a blind spot in the method.
  • Used inside the volatility forecast, the anomaly score as a HAR correction improves on HAR by 2.5% (1 day) and 1.4% (5 days), but it ties with bias-corrected HAR (p = 0.99 and 0.55). A signature-free detector does the same.
  • One lead: at 5 days, every detector built on time-of-day-adjusted price plus spread held up on the last fold (0.51–0.54), while plain price fell to 0.45–0.48. Not significant on one pair, but worth testing on others.
Next

What we would try next

Where a gain is still plausible

  • Longer horizons. On Bitcoin the gain grew with the horizon (+4.6% at 1 day, +15.4% at 30 days). This EUR/USD run stopped at 5 days; a 22-day run is the most direct next test.
  • More pairs, pooled. One pair gives about 3,300 training days for ~800 signature features. Pooling GBP/USD, USD/JPY and AUD/USD multiplies the data and tests the spread lead above.
  • Event information. FX volatility is driven by scheduled releases (payrolls, central-bank meetings) that no price path anticipates. A release calendar is the obvious missing input.

What we would drop

  • Neural networks on signatures: far worse than ridge on both assets.
  • Time-of-day adjustment for the forecasting path: keep the original channels.
  • Signature features in a direction classifier: they overfit; use the volatility forecast instead.
Method
Data
EUR/USD hourly bid and ask candles from Dukascopy, 92,480 trading hours (Jan 2012 – Oct 2026), median spread 0.28 bp. FX days run 22:00–22:00 UTC.
Path
The last 22 trading days of hourly data. Depth-3 signature with time and lead-lag. Channels: time-of-day-adjusted price and range, activity, abnormal spread. Original channels for comparison.
Target
Realized volatility over the next 1 or 5 trading days, about 3,300 forecast days.
Testing
3 expanding walk-forward folds, purged so no training target overlaps a test day. Settings fixed in advance. Diebold-Mariano tests for forecasts, block bootstrap for AUCs.
Benchmarks
HAR-RV with a leverage term; HAR with its bias removed; HAR on hourly realized variance; signature-free features from the same window (matched lag bank, multiscale statistics).
Bitcoin
Same pipeline on BTC/USDT (daily rows since 2015, hourly path since 2017), from the earlier notebooks.
