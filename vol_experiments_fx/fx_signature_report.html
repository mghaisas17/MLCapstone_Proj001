Do path signatures carry over from Bitcoin to FX?

On Bitcoin, signatures of the hourly price path improved volatility forecasts by 5–15% over the standard HAR model, and a signature anomaly detector passed our test at one day. We ran the same experiments on four major currency pairs to see whether those results carry over.

Partly. The method carries over; the gain over HAR does not.

On FX the signature is again a much better summary of the hourly path than the raw path itself, but it does not beat a HAR forecast. The reason looks like the data, not the method: on FX, HAR fed with hourly realized variance doesn't beat plain daily HAR either, while on Bitcoin it does. At 1 and 5 days the FX hourly path holds little that the daily volatility history doesn't already contain. Combining pairs does not change that.

The test that explains it: does the hourly data help HAR itself?

The one EUR/USD model that does beat HAR at 1 day (simple multiscale statistics, +2.8%) is no better than HAR with its bias removed (+2.5%, p = 0.65). What it improves is the forecast's average level, not its timing.

On both assets the same information fed in as raw lags does 6–14% worse, so the signature's way of summarizing a path helps on both. What changes on FX is that the path itself carries little forecasting information. A neural network on the signature did much worse than ridge on both assets (−59% and −42% versus HAR on EUR/USD).

The new FX channels made EUR/USD forecasts slightly worse

Finding 5 · Anomaly detection

Signature anomaly scores flag Bitcoin surprises at 1 day, not EUR/USD ones

A day counts as a surprise when realized volatility lands in the top 20% relative to the HAR forecast, so a detector earns credit only for what HAR missed. The best signature detector, picked on the first two folds, must beat the best signature-free detector on the last fold.

Best signature detectorBest signature-free detector

Surprise AUC on the held-out fold; 0.5 = no information beyond HAR. Only Bitcoin at 1 day passes (block bootstrap p = 0.04). EUR/USD: p = 0.50 (1 day) and 0.18 (5 days).

  • The detectors can see path shapes: on synthetic windows where only the order of returns is changed, they score AUC 0.86–0.95. The failure is in the data, not a blind spot in the method.
  • Used inside the volatility forecast, the anomaly score improves on HAR by 2.5% (1 day) and 1.4% (5 days) but ties with bias-corrected HAR (p = 0.99 and 0.55). A signature-free detector does the same.
Finding 6 · Four pairs, and pairs together

The result holds on four pairs, and adding other pairs to the path makes it worse

We repeated the forecasts on GBP/USD, USD/JPY and AUD/USD. Then we gave each pair's signature extra channels: a dollar factor (the dollar's average move against all four pairs), or the other three pairs' hourly prices, so the signature's cross terms could pick up how pairs move together and which one moves first.

Worse in 18 of 24 combinations, significantly in 13. EUR/USD + GBP/USD, the most natural pairing, hurts EUR/USD. The only gains are on GBP/USD, none significant.

  • No pair beats HAR with its bias removed, in any variant.
  • Why adding pairs hurts. Most of what the other pairs carry is the dollar move, which the pair's own price already shows, and each channel adds hundreds of signature features (about 400 own, 800 with one more, 2,400 with three) on about 3,300 training days. Smaller additions hurt less, but none helps.
  • The signature still beats raw lags given the same multi-pair data (+0.7% to +4.5%, significant at 1 day on GBP/USD, USD/JPY and AUD/USD).
Robustness

One extreme event can wreck a signature model: the Brexit vote

Our first GBP/USD run was 36–51% worse than HAR. The largest errors all fall in the weeks after the 23 June 2016 vote: the 22-day window still contained the shock, and the signature model forecast four to six times the volatility that followed.

Next

What we would try next

Where a gain is still plausible

  • Longer horizons. On Bitcoin the gain grew with the horizon (+4.6% at 1 day, +15.4% at 30 days). The FX runs stop at 5 days; a 22-day run is the most direct next test.
  • Pooling pairs. Each pair gives about 3,300 training days. Training one model on all four pairs multiplies the data, which Finding 6 suggests is a binding limit.
  • Event information. FX volatility is driven by scheduled releases (payrolls, central-bank meetings) that no price path anticipates. A release calendar is the obvious missing input.

What we would drop

  • Neural networks on signatures: far worse than ridge on both assets.
  • Time-of-day adjustment for the forecasting path: no consistent gain across pairs.
  • Other pairs' prices or a dollar factor in the path: worse on every pair.
  • Signature features in a direction classifier: they overfit; use the volatility forecast instead.
Method
Data
Hourly bid and ask candles from Dukascopy for EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY and AUD/USD, Jan 2012 – Oct 2026 (92,480 trading hours for EUR/USD, median spread 0.28 bp). FX days run 22:00–22:00 UTC.
Path
The last 22 trading days of hourly data. Depth-3 signature with time and lead-lag. Original channels (price, activity, range); EUR/USD also with time-of-day-adjusted price and range and the abnormal spread.
Target
Realized volatility over the next 1 or 5 trading days, about 3,300 forecast days per pair.
Testing
3 expanding walk-forward folds, purged so no training target overlaps a test day. Settings fixed in advance. Diebold-Mariano tests for forecasts, block bootstrap for AUCs.
Benchmarks
HAR-RV with a leverage term; HAR with its bias removed; HAR on hourly realized variance; signature-free features from the same window (matched lag bank, multiscale statistics).
Pairs
Multi-pair runs add the other pairs' hourly log prices (all three, or one at a time) or a dollar factor as extra path channels, with standardized ridge inputs capped at ±5 SD.
Bitcoin
Same pipeline on BTC/USDT (daily rows since 2015, hourly path since 2017), from the earlier notebooks.
